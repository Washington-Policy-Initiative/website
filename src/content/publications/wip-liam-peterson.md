abstract: "Washington State's school librarian workforce has declined sharply over the past decade, even as statewide reading achievement has fallen. Using district-level staffing and assessment data from the Office of Superintendent of Public Instruction (OSPI) and locale classifications from the National Center for Education Statistics (NCES), this paper documents school librarian access and reading achievement across Washington's 298 school districts from 2015–16 through 2024–25. Full-time equivalent (FTE) librarian positions fell by nearly 43% over the period, from 1,100.41 to 632.49, and by 2024–25, 64% of districts employed no certified teacher-librarian. Grouping districts into six locale tiers, a chi-square test finds that librarian access is strongly associated with geography (χ² = 92.5, p < 0.0001): rural and distant rural districts, which also post the lowest reading results, are the least likely to employ a librarian. The paper does not establish a causal link between librarian staffing and reading outcomes. Instead, it documents a concurrent decline in both and places it in the context of prior research, including Coker's 2015 study of Washington school libraries, and of RCW 28A.320.240, which leaves library staffing to district discretion. It concludes by recommending a statewide standard requiring every K–12 district to employ at least one certified teacher-librarian."
