abstract: "Digital advertising has become a major vehicle for electioneering communication, creating new challenges for campaign finance transparency. Washington State's Fair Campaign Practices Act (FCPA), among the strictest disclosure laws in the nation, requires both political candidates and commercial advertisers to disclose financial and distribution information about political advertisements. Repeated violations by major technology companies, including Google and Meta, have raised questions about whether online platforms can comply with these requirements. This paper examines Washington's campaign finance disclosure laws, the legal disputes involving digital advertising platforms, and how disclosure requirements differ across states. Through an analysis of existing legislation and state-by-state comparisons, it identifies significant gaps in the regulation of digital political advertising nationwide. The findings suggest that Washington's strict requirements are not the primary source of these conflicts; rather, the conflicts reflect the absence of consistent federal standards for digital advertising transparency. The paper recommends more consistent disclosure requirements across states and stronger transparency measures for digital platforms, so that voters can access accurate information about who funds political advertisements."
